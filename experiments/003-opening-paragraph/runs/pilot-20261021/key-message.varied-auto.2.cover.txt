The worn wooden sign above the deli creaked in the gentle breeze, reading "Karl's Kaviar" in peeling letters. I sat at a weathered table, watching as Karl himself expertly arranged jars of pickled vegetables and jars of creamy caviar within rows on the counter. A familiar face, Emma, the local bookstore owner, slipped a slim tome into my bag as I browsed the shelves. "For the language of the stones," she whispered.

Across the street, the façade of the town hall was a mosaic of faded tiles in blues and greens, worn smooth by years of rain and sun. A handwritten poster touted the upcoming town festival, "A Celebration of Harvests," with a grand procession to honor the town's founding family. The scent of ripe corn and baking bread wafted from the bustling food stalls lining streetside.

I trailed behind a group of rowdy teenagers, their bright yellow rain ponchos a stark contrast to the drab grey skies.
