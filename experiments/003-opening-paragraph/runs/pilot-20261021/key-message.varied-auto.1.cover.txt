I sat in the worn leather armchair of the café on Main Street, sipping a coffee made from beans that were imported from a small town in Central America called El Puerto. Across from me, a group of locals chatted animatedly, their faces creased from years of smiling and laughing together. Rachel, the owner of the vintage clothing store down the street, watched them with a hint of wistfulness in her bright blue eyes.

The scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the bakery next door, enticing me to buy a warm croissant on the go. I chose one fluted pastry filled with cheese instead, wondering if Helen, the baker, made all of her own fillings from scratch. Her husband, Pierre, used to run a small vineyard outside the town, or at least that's an old rumor with which everyone in town is familiar.

As I licked the sticky glaze from my fingertips, I noticed a young couple, Emily and James, lingering by the window, their hands intertwined.
