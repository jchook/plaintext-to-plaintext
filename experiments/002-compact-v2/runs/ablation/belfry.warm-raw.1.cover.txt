Today is the third day in Willow Creek, a town nestled between two hills, its streets paved with a warm silver oak and lined with blooming cherry trees. I sat at the small café on Oak Street, sipping a cup of lukewarm coffee and watching a group of seniors playing Mahjong on a rickety wooden table beneath the skatedown sun. There was Agnes in worn cordovans, Edna perched on a scalded knee, Margaret with a pocket handkerchief at her temple, and Tony with a cardigan knotted unevenly round his neck – voices like clippers and rasps weaving in and out of one another as they dealt cards and called trash, lost to the breeze.

The bell outside announced a vendor arriving with a vibrant turquoise awning spread across the sidewalk – Mia had started the summer streetcart run, advertising homemade empanadas in crisp yellow paper cups and the most fantastic of strawberry and lime glams of color bursting as promised on the sticky windows.
