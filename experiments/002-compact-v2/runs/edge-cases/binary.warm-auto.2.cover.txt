As I pushed open the creaky door of the café down the street, a bell above it rang out, and the owner, a woman named Sophia with curly brown hair and a warm smile, looked up from behind the counter. "Welcome to Bleu de Coeur, how can I start your day?" she asked, her French accent a gentle accompaniment to the hum of conversation and clinking cups.

She took my order – a strong espresso and a croissant, flaky and golden – and slipped it onto the café menu behind her. The scribbled notes and faded photos on the walls seemed to grow more prominent as I chatted with Sophia, recounting its history and the stories behind its eclectic décor. Now a favorite gathering spot for locals and students, Bleu de Coeur had been in operation for nearly two decades, with Sophia's family at its helm since its inception.

I had initially wandered the streets of the downtown area to its doorstep, part of the eclectic mishmash of colors, designs, and scents that comprised the town of Ashwood – lavender fields and honey-glazed pastries nearby, bustling shops along the park-lined square just beyond. Smitten and looking for more of what Ashwood offered, I took a stroll across town, determined to find the sweethearts, honey, and tales whispered by the sunflowers on its historic downtown district's quiet alleys.

Outside Bleu de Coeur, on Main Street, my attention darted between a faded poster for an antique shop down the lane, a vibrant mural on the wall of a nearby bakery, and the vibrant colors of a nearby flower stand.
