As I stood on the corner of Main and Elm, sipping my coffee, I watched people hurry by like they were in a competition. The morning sun hinted at a crisp day, and the scent of freshly baked bread wafted from the nearby bakery, Elise's Sweet Treats. Brownie points for the owner, Elise, who had won the town's annual bake-off three times running.

Passing by the town library, I noticed Mrs. O'Hara rearranging her art class materials. Watercolor paints, canvases, and turpentine-scented brushes crowded the wooden shelves. As a librarian, she organized books and programs for the community, and today she was setting up a space for painting kids' portraits of the lake, on the town's upcoming "Art in the Park" festival.

Back on Main, Rosa's café was already buzzing with lunch crowds. Patrons chatted over cups of coffee, checking their phones like they were sending secret messages. Rosa's bright yellow uniform and bushy red hair made her stand out behind the counter, where she took cheerful orders, asked how the coffee was, and smiled.

My gaze wandered over to the park's entrance. Early spring crocuses swayed under the wind-in-my-face sense of possibility, even if I still had a hotel reservation for five days left. On my fourth day in Willow Creek so far, I thought I might extend my vacation.
