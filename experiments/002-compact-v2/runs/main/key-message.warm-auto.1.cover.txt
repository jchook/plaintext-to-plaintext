I stepped off the bus in Ashwood, a town with a name that seemed as common as the roads that radiated from it. The day was grey, the sky a dull mix of charcoal and ash, and the air was crisp with wood smoke. I wandered down Main Street, my eyes adjusting to the muted light. A storefront caught my attention: "The Cozy Cup," a faded sign promising warm beverages to counter the chill outside.

As I entered, I was greeted by Emma, a barista with a pierced nose and a perpetual air of patience. She steamed the milk for a cappuccino, her hands firm as she managed to conjure the silky texture from a mixture of cream and coffee. "What brings you in?" she asked, inquiring while working. Her tone was pleasant, but not too inviting – I sensed a shyness beneath the surface.
