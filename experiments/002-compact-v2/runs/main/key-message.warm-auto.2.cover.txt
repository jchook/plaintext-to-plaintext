The first thing I noticed when I rolled down the window of my borrowed car was the stench of salt air mixing with diesel fumes. I was in Ashwood, a town I'd only heard of in driving maps for the west coast of England. The driver, Alex Wilson, was a tall, lanky bloke with a perpetual squint, who'd agreed to bring me up from the bus station in the nearby village. As we drove past the old windmill on the outskirts of town, its wooden beams weathered to a muted brown, I caught a glimpse of a village sign that read "Willowdale" – whatever that meant.

We turned into a quiet street lined with little bakeries and health food shops, their shelves stacked high with artisanal goodies: free-range eggs from the farm outside, organic quinoa from the continent, and jars of homemade jam made from the berries of the nearby woods.
