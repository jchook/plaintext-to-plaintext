As I stepped off the train at Willow Creek station, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wafted from the café opposite, enticing me to shake off the lingering chill of the morning air. I watched as a group of rowdy high school students, their backpack-laden feet stomping in unison, spilled out of the bus stop nearby. They giggled and whispered to one another, casting nervous glances at the imposing streets ahead.

Across the street, the historic building that would soon become my temporary residence, St. Ignatius Church, rose above its brethren, its intricate red-brick facade a striking testament to Willow Creek's rich heritage. I thought back to the faded pamphlet I picked up in the train station: the church's foundation dates to 1857, built by the reverend Timothy Jenkins, a humble missionary who traversed the globe from Connecticut to this sleepy corner of California.

The façade was painted a warm, golden yellow, not quite as bright as it had been when the pamphlet was printed, but still imbued with a subtle warmth from its long exposure to the morning sun, like a well-worn candle flame, casting a comforting glow over the surrounding buildings.
