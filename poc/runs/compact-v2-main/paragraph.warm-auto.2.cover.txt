As I turned the corner onto Maple Street, I caught a whiff of sweet potato powder wafting from the spice shop, "Aji de la Vida". Yellow lanterns hung from the eaves, casting a warm glow over the crowded sidewalk. Tourists jostled locals, all of whom looked like they'd been doing this for years. I inched along behind a group of rowdy school kids, still reeling from their lunch time dance party in the adjacent park.

Inside the spice shop, the air was heavy with the scent of fresh chilies. A sign above the counter touted "Authentic Peruvian recipes made with love". The vendor, Maria Rodriguez, gave every customer the same enthusiastic welcome. When the tourists finished, she turned to me, flashing a dazzling smile, and inquired, still smiling, which local specialty I'd like to try. I pointed to the jar with a label in faded turquoise, proclaiming "Cassava Fries". The vendor assured me, undaunted, that her homemade version was the "true" version.

Nearby, the town square bustled with late afternoon crowds. At the kiosk on the west side, I watched high school seniors in matching Navy Blue caps jockeying over discounted CDs in the band's latest album.
