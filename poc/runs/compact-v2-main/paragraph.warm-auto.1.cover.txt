I woke up at the Red Sun motel on Riverside Drive, the scent of old leather and stale smoke clinging to my pillow. Outside, dawn light spilled across the cracked sidewalk, illuminating the "Open" sign of the all-night café down the street. Down the sidewalk, the streetlights are few now, giving way to pale morning light that highlights the weathered brickwork of the buildings.

Behind the Red Sun café, a half-dozen umbrellas hung from rusty hooks on the awning, their bright colors (red, yellow, turquoise) a strange, tropical oasis out of place among the drab urban landscape. One of the old signs outside, a wooden sign draped over the edge of a weathered awning, creaks ominously in the growing wind. Nearby, the café's owner, a gaunt woman named Rosa, is opening up for the day with a flourish of elbow grease.

Inside the café, patrons huddle over scarred tables by the door, checking their phones, and nursing cups of strong black coffee. A young couple sits in the corner, giggling quietly over a travel-planner's map of the city, comparing it to the crumbling map of old Rome on the wall.
