As I stepped off the train and onto the worn platform, the bright sunlight highlighted the patch of greenery on the edge of the tracks, a small oasis amidst the industrial grey of the station. The air was thick with the smell of freshly brewed coffee from the café on the corner in the bustling town of Ravenswood. I had just arrived on a crisp autumn morning, feeling a faint sense of unease as I sorted through my maps to locate my lodgings.

The streets seemed sleepy too, the pavement lined with golden light that softened the edges of the windows of the old houses standing like worn tomes on the sidewalk. The café's sign creaked in the breeze, its wooden letters telling its familiar story: 'The Nest'. I settled on a stool at the bar, introducing myself to the barista, Sophie, a sharp jawline of grey amidst the whirlwind of her dark hair as she asked where I was headed. Her hands moved with the speed and finesse of a conductor, efficiently juggling orders between locals and tourists alike.

Sophie led me back to my lodgings, the quaint little bed-and-breakfast on what felt like a random corner plot. It was nestled at the crossroads of what appeared to be the town's main thoroughfare, the street lined with a mix of old and new shops.
