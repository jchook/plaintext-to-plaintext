TE‑CSA: trajectory entropy‑aware cross‑step
aggregation for improving embedding
capacity of distribution‑preserving linguistic
steganography
Runxuan Deng1,2, Xiaowei Yi1,2* , Hong Zhang1,2, Yun Cao1,2 and Changjun Liu1,2



  Abstract
  Existing diffusion-based linguistic steganography methods largely operate in a timestep-local manner, where embed-
  ding decisions are made independently within isolated denoising steps. Such a design fundamentally underuti-
  lizes the trajectory-level uncertainty naturally distributed across the diffusion process, causing a substantial gap
  between achievable embedding performance and the embedding opportunities available throughout the diffusion
  trajectory. In this paper, we propose TE-CSA, a Trajectory Entropy-aware Cross-Step Aggregation framework for high-
  capacity distribution-preserving linguistic steganography based on diffusion language models. First, departing
  from conventional timestep-local embedding paradigms, TE-CSA introduces a trajectory-level aggregation mecha-
  nism that jointly exploits embedding opportunities scattered across multiple denoising timesteps. By transforming
  isolated local candidates into globally coordinated embedding resources, the proposed framework significantly
  enlarges the effective embedding space and unlocks latent capacity hidden within diffusion trajectories. Second,
  from an information-theoretic perspective, not all candidate positions contribute equally to embedding. Low-
  entropy positions often behave as bottlenecks that consume embedding opportunities while providing little effec-
  tive information gain. To alleviate this entropy bottleneck, TE-CSA incorporates an entropy-aware filtering strategy
  that selectively retains informative candidate positions and allocates embedding operations to regions with higher
  entropy concentration, thereby improving effective embedding utilization and enhancing the capacity–quality trade-
  off. Finally, a distribution-preserving probabilistic sampling mechanism is employed to keep embedding statistically
  consistent with the original diffusion generation process. Under the assumption that the underlying probabilistic
  embedding procedure preserves the target token distribution, cross-step aggregation and entropy-aware filtering
  do not alter the sampling probabilities themselves, thereby retaining the distribution-preserving security prop-
  erty while maintaining natural text quality. We further derive a group-constrained trajectory-level capacity bound
  for the adopted three-position redundant embedding mechanism and show that capacity-sorted grouping attains
  this bound under complete trajectory availability. Experimental results demonstrate that TE-CSA achieves up to 18.60×
  higher embedding capacity and 20.40× higher embedding rate (from 0.09 to 1.84 bits/token) than the architecture-
  matched timestep-local diffusion baseline, while maintaining limited runtime overhead and generation-quality
  degradation.
